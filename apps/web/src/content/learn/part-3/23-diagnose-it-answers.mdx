Keep your notes and your trace open while you compare these answers with
the ones you wrote in
[Diagnosing a request from its trace](/learn/part-3/22-diagnose-it). If
you have not tried the exercise yet, go back and do it first. The
comparison is worth far more once you have formed your own explanation.

As a reminder, the mixed scenario runs four rules on one `expense` event:

- A and B post to `/ok`.
- C posts to `/slow?ms=15000`, past the 10 second run timeout.
- D posts to `/fail?status=500`.

Your trace ids and exact timings will differ from these. What should match
are the outcomes, the relative durations and the way the spans overlap.

## The slowest operation

The longest `Engine.execute` span belongs to rule C and lasts about 10
seconds. The receiver was set to answer after 15 seconds, but the run's
timeout interrupts the POST before that answer arrives. Because the
timeout sits inside the execution span, the span covers the whole wait and
ends with `TimeoutError`.

The recorded result agrees. C's span has status Error, and its row in
`runs list` reads `failed (timeout): timed out after 10s`. Together, the
duration and the reason explain why the run ended after about 10 seconds
instead of waiting the full 15.

## The failed operation

D failed differently: it received a 500 response. Its `Engine.execute`
span has status Error with the message
`POST http://localhost:4600/fail?status=500 answered 500`, and its run row
reads `failed (status)`, with the 500 in the error details.

C failed as well, so naming both C and D is also correct, as long as you
separated their reasons. D got a response outside the 2xx range. C got no
response before its timeout.

## The work that completed

A and B are the successful runs, and both records say so: their run rows
show `succeeded` and their execution spans have status Ok.

To see whether they waited, compare the start times. All four
`Engine.execute` spans start within moments of each other under the same
`Engine.handleEvent` parent. With concurrency set to 4, none of the four
rules had to wait for another to finish. A, B and D are done long before
C, so C's slow request delays none of their outcomes.

The CLI does not return as soon as A and B finish, though. The event
request waits for all four results, so it takes about 10 seconds, the
length of C's timeout rather than the sum of the four runs. The
`Engine.handleEvent` duration and the elapsed time of `events submit` both
show that.

## Evidence of the timeout

Two places confirm that C hit the timeout. Its execution span lasts about
10 seconds and ends with `TimeoutError`, and its run row records the
reason `timeout`. Those are two independent records of the same ending. If
you relied on the duration alone, add the error or the reason to your
notes: a request can take about 10 seconds without timing out, and it is
the recorded error and reason that say why this one stopped.

## Check the complete result

Put the observations together and you have four run rows and four
execution spans, matching the previous lesson's checkpoint. Two spans are
red, C for the timeout and D for the 500 response, and the other two have
status Ok. The rows explain the outcomes, and the overlapping spans show
that the rules ran concurrently.

You have followed one expense through four independent actions and
explained both the results and the time spent waiting. The history tells
us what Reflex recorded, and the trace shows how the work overlapped.
Keeping both is what makes that explanation possible after the request has
ended.
