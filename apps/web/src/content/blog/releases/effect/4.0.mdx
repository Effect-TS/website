## One ecosystem, zero dependencies

Many previously separate packages now live in `effect` itself. The packages around it share a single version and release in lockstep.

The core `effect` package has **zero runtime dependencies**. We control every line we ship, so there are no third-party dependency chains and less exposure to supply chain attacks.

## From functions to clusters

Effect started with a much smaller scope. Today, it covers the full range, from a single function to a distributed system.

People came for the programming model: an effect system with built-in typed errors, dependency injection, resource management, structured concurrency, and observability. The standard library and platform integrations grew around it. Then came higher-level primitives for distributed systems, including durable workflows and clustering.

Every layer is built on that same model, with the same guarantees all the way up.

## Long-term support

Teams are betting their products on Effect, and they deserve commitments they can plan around. Starting with this release, **every major version of Effect has a long-term support policy.**

For Effect 4.x, that means:

- Bug fixes until September 2029, or one year after 5.0 ships, whichever is later
- Security fixes until September 2029, or two years after 5.0 ships, whichever is later

## What's next

Our first priority is stabilizing more of the ecosystem. Some of the newer modules are still tagged `@stability unstable` or `@stability experimental`, which means they may change in minor or patch releases. We'll use production feedback to refine them and promote them to stable.

Beyond that, we'll continue expanding Effect into a single programming model for your entire application, with broader native platform support and more higher-level primitives.
