These are the answers to
[Diagnosing a request from its trace](/learn/part-3/22-diagnose-it).
If you have not written yours yet, go back and do that first. Comparing is
more useful than reading.

The mixed scenario runs four rules on one `expense` event:

- A and B post to `/ok`.
- C posts to `/slow?ms=15000`, past the 10 second run timeout.
- D posts to `/fail?status=500`.

Your trace ids and times differ, but the shape does not.

## 1. The slowest operation

C's `Engine.execute` span. Its duration equals the configured timeout, 10
seconds. The timeout runs inside the span and stops the POST there. The
receiver would have taken 15 seconds.

The span's status is Error, with the `TimeoutError` message. C's row in
`runs list` is `failed (timeout): timed out after 10s`.

## 2. The failed operation

D's `Engine.execute` span. Its status is Error, with the message
`POST http://localhost:4600/fail?status=500 answered 500`. D's row is
`failed (status)`, and the 500 is in its error.

C failed too, by timing out. Counting both is a good answer, as long as you
tell them apart. D got an answer it did not want. C got no answer in time.

## 3. The work that completed

A's and B's rows are `succeeded`, and their spans are Ok.

All four `Engine.execute` spans start within milliseconds of each other,
under the same `Engine.handleEvent` parent. The event ran them at once, at
most four at a time. A, B and D finished long before C, and nothing waited
for C.

The whole request took about 10 seconds, the timeout, not the sum of the
four. You can read that from the `Engine.handleEvent` span, or from how long
`events submit` took.

## 4. The timeout

Yes, it fired, for C. Two places show it: C's span lasts as long as the
timeout, and its row's reason is `timeout`.

## The overall shape

Two red spans, two Ok, four rows. Those are the same numbers as the previous
lesson's checkpoint.
