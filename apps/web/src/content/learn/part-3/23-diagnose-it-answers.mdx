These are the answers to [lesson 22](/learn/part-3/22-diagnose-it). If you
haven't written yours yet, go back and do that first. Comparing is much more
useful than reading.

As a reminder, the mixed scenario runs four rules on one `expense` event:

- A and B post to `/ok`.
- C posts to `/slow?ms=15000`, past the 10 second run timeout.
- D posts to `/fail?status=500`.

Your trace ids and times will differ, but the shape won't.

## 1. Slowest operation

C's `Engine.execute` span. Its duration equals the configured timeout, 10
seconds. That's because the timeout runs inside the span and stops the POST
there. The receiver would have taken 15.

The span's status is Error, with the `TimeoutError` message. C's row in
`runs list` is `failed (timeout): timed out after 10s`.

## 2. Failed operation

D's `Engine.execute` span. Its status is Error, with the message
`POST http://localhost:4600/fail?status=500 answered 500`. D's row is
`failed (status)`, and the 500 is in its error.

C failed too, by timing out. Counting both is a good answer, as long as you
tell them apart. D got an answer it didn't want. C got no answer in time.

## 3. Independent work completed

A's and B's rows are `succeeded`, and their spans are Ok.

All four `Engine.execute` spans start within milliseconds of each other, under
the same `Engine.handleEvent` parent. The event ran them at once, at most four
at a time. A, B and D finished long before C, and nothing waited for C.

The whole request took about 10 seconds, the timeout, not the sum of the four.
You can read that from the `Engine.handleEvent` span, or from how long
`events submit` took.

## 4. Timeout fired

Yes, for C. Two places show it: C's span lasts as long as the timeout, and its
row's reason is `timeout`.

## The overall shape

Two red spans, two Ok, four rows. Those are the same numbers as the lesson 21
checkpoint.
