If you've spent time in the Effect community over the past year, you've probably heard of [Foldkit](https://foldkit.dev/). Devin Jameson started working on it in June 2025 as a side project to explore what an Elm-inspired frontend framework built with Effect could look like. It caught on fast. Teams started shipping production apps with it, and Foldkit has since grown to more than 1,200 GitHub stars and around 180,000 weekly npm downloads.

Devin built all of that on nights and weekends, outside of his day job. Watching Foldkit take off, we couldn't help wondering what he could build if this were his actual job. Today Devin is becoming a core maintainer of Effect, joining us full time at Effectful Technologies, and bringing Foldkit under the Effect organization.

## What comes next

You can already use Effect across the stack, from backend services and HTTP APIs to the browser with libraries like Effect Atom. Even so, Effect still feels more like a toolkit than a framework. You get solid building blocks, but wiring them into a full application is still up to you.

That's what we're setting out to change. For years, people have asked what an end-to-end Effect application framework would look like if the whole thing were built to fit together from day one. Your API, your database, and your UI, all speaking the same language. Errors typed from the server straight to the button a user clicks. Every layer testable on its own, and an architecture that still holds up two years later when the app is ten times bigger. Now that Devin is on the core team, we're finally building it.

This matters even more as coding agents write and refactor more of our code. Agents do far better with real guardrails in place. When an app's behavior is spelled out in the types and follows one consistent model across client and server, there's much less room for them to drift or break things silently.

Welcome to the team, Devin. Now let's get to work.
